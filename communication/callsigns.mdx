A callsign is your radio identity. It tells dispatch and every other unit on the channel who you are, what role you fill, and — for law enforcement — what your badge number is. Using the correct callsign format is not optional: dispatch assigns calls by callsign, units request backup by callsign, and logs are built around them. Learn your callsign before your first shift and use it exactly as formatted every time you key up.

## Law enforcement callsigns

### Standard patrol units

Law enforcement callsigns follow a **unit-type prefix + badge or unit number** format. The prefix identifies the assignment category; the number identifies you specifically.

| Prefix | Assignment | Example callsign |
| ------ | ---------- | ---------------- |
| `Sam` | SBCSD patrol (sheriff) | Sam-7 |
| `Charlie` | CHP patrol | Charlie-14 |
| `Lincoln` | SBCSD supervisor | Lincoln-1 |
| `Metro` | CHP supervisor | Metro-3 |

<Note>
  Your callsign prefix is assigned at the time you are badged into your division. Do not self-assign a prefix. If you are unsure of your correct prefix, confirm with a supervisor before going 10-8.
</Note>

### Specialized division callsigns

Specialized units carry a division identifier as part of their callsign. The format is **division abbreviation + unit number**. On air, the division abbreviation is spoken as a word or spelled phonetically as shown below.

| Division | Abbreviation | Spoken form | Example callsign |
| -------- | ------------ | ----------- | ---------------- |
| Highway Safety Division | HSD | "Hotel-Sierra-Delta" | HSD-4 |
| K9 Unit | K9 | "K-Nine" | K9-2 |
| Emergency Response Team | ERT | "Echo-Romeo-Tango" | ERT-6 |
| Criminal Investigations Bureau | CIB | "Sierra-India-Bravo" | CIB-11 |
| Air Operations Division | AOD | "Alpha-Oscar-Delta" | AOD-1 |

<Note>
  When identifying yourself on a shared channel, state your full callsign including the division prefix — "K9-2" not just "Two." Other units may share the same number under a different prefix.
</Note>

## Fire department callsigns

Fire callsigns identify the **apparatus**, not the person operating it. When a firefighter transmits on the radio, they speak as the unit — Engine One, not "Firefighter Smith." This convention applies at all times: if the apparatus is on scene, it is on the air.

### Apparatus callsigns

| Prefix | Apparatus type | Example callsign | Spoken form |
| ------ | -------------- | ---------------- | ----------- |
| `E` | Engine | E1 | "Engine One" |
| `L` | Ladder | L2 | "Ladder Two" |
| `R` | Rescue | R1 | "Rescue One" |
| `T` | Tanker | T3 | "Tanker Three" |
| `M` | Medic (EMS unit) | M2 | "Medic Two" |
| `BC` | Battalion Chief | BC1 | "Battalion One" |

<Note>
  Always speak the full designation — "Engine One," not just "E1." On a shared channel with law enforcement, single letters followed by numbers are ambiguous. The spoken form removes any doubt.
</Note>

### Fire command callsigns

When a scene command post is established, the command callsign takes the name of the incident location: **"Baseline Command," "Court Street Command,"** and so on. Units on scene report to Command; Command reports to dispatch.

## Dispatch callsigns

Dispatch is addressed as **"Dispatch"** or **"Central"** depending on channel configuration. Either term is acceptable. In all cases, address dispatch first before stating your callsign.

```text
// Correct — addressing dispatch, then identifying yourself
"Dispatch, Sam-7, 10-8."

// Also correct on channels using Central
"Central, Engine One, on scene at 485 West 5th Street."
```

<Warning>
  Do not address dispatch as "radio," "base," or any other informal term. "Dispatch" and "Central" are the only accepted identifiers. Non-standard addressing creates confusion on multi-agency channels.
</Warning>

## Callsign rules summary

<Note>
  The following rules apply to all members on all channels at all times:

  - Always state your **full callsign** when initiating a transmission — never just a number.
  - **Do not share or borrow** another unit's callsign. Each callsign must map to exactly one person or apparatus at any given time.
  - If your callsign changes mid-shift (e.g. you take command of an incident), **advise dispatch** before using the new identifier.
  - Fire units speak as the **apparatus**; law enforcement units speak as **themselves**.
  - When working a joint incident, **confirm callsigns with all agencies** on scene at the start of the operation.
</Note>
