A callsign is your radio identity. It tells dispatch and every other unit on the channel who you are and, for law enforcement, which agency you belong to. Using the correct callsign format is not optional: dispatch assigns calls by callsign, units request backup by callsign, and logs are built around them. Learn your callsign before your first shift and use it exactly as formatted every time you key up.

## Law enforcement callsigns

### Agency callsign format

Each law enforcement member holds one callsign, assigned when you are added to the roster. The format tells you the agency at a glance.

| Agency | Format | Example callsign | Spoken form |
| ------ | ------ | ---------------- | ----------- |
| CHP | 3 digits | `104` | "One-Zero-Four" |
| SBCSD | 2 digits + 1 letter | `12A` | "One-Two-Adam" |

- Speak each digit individually: `104` is "One-Zero-Four," never "One-Oh-Four" or "One Hundred Four."
- Speak the SBCSD letter with the law enforcement phonetic alphabet: `12A` is "One-Two-Adam," `27B` is "Two-Seven-Boy." See [Phonetics](/communication/phonetics).
- Supervisors and command staff use the callsign assigned to them on the roster. There is no separate supervisor prefix.

<Note>
  Your callsign is assigned by a supervisor and listed on your agency's roster ([CHP roster](/chp/roster), [SBCSD roster](/sbcsd/roster)). Do not self-assign a callsign. If you are unsure of yours, confirm with a supervisor before going 10-8.
</Note>

### Specialized division callsigns

Specialized units add a division identifier in front of their agency callsign. The format is **division abbreviation + your callsign**. On air, the division abbreviation is spoken as a word or spelled phonetically as shown below.

| Division | Abbreviation | Spoken form | Example callsign |
| -------- | ------------ | ----------- | ---------------- |
| Highway Safety Division | HSD | "Hotel-Sierra-Delta" | HSD-104 |
| K9 Unit | K9 | "K-Nine" | K9-12A |
| Emergency Response Team | ERT | "Echo-Romeo-Tango" | ERT-31C |
| Criminal Investigations Bureau | CIB | "Sierra-India-Bravo" | CIB-22B |
| Air Operations Division | AOD | "Alpha-Oscar-Delta" | AOD-15A |

<Note>
  When identifying yourself on a shared channel, state your full callsign including the division prefix — "K9-12A," not just "12A." Dispatch uses the prefix to know which resource is responding.
</Note>

## Fire department callsigns

Fire callsigns identify the **apparatus**, not the person operating it. When a firefighter transmits on the radio, they speak as the unit — Engine One, not "Firefighter Smith." This convention applies at all times: if the apparatus is on scene, it is on the air.

### Apparatus callsigns

| Prefix | Apparatus type | Example callsign | Spoken form |
| ------ | -------------- | ---------------- | ----------- |
| `E` | Engine | E1 | "Engine One" |
| `L` | Ladder | L2 | "Ladder Two" |
| `R` | Rescue | R1 | "Rescue One" |
| `T` | Tanker | T3 | "Tanker Three" |
| `M` | Medic (EMS unit) | M2 | "Medic Two" |
| `BC` | Battalion Chief | BC1 | "Battalion One" |

<Note>
  Always speak the full designation — "Engine One," not just "E1." On a shared channel with law enforcement, single letters followed by numbers are ambiguous. The spoken form removes any doubt.
</Note>

### Fire command callsigns

When a scene command post is established, the command callsign takes the name of the incident location: **"Baseline Command," "Court Street Command,"** and so on. Units on scene report to Command; Command reports to dispatch.

## Dispatch callsigns

Dispatch is addressed as **"Dispatch"** or **"Central"** depending on channel configuration. Either term is acceptable. In all cases, address dispatch first before stating your callsign.

```text
// Correct — addressing dispatch, then identifying yourself
"Dispatch, 12A, 10-8."

// Also correct on channels using Central
"Central, Engine One, on scene at 485 West 5th Street."
```

<Warning>
  Do not address dispatch as "radio," "base," or any other informal term. "Dispatch" and "Central" are the only accepted identifiers. Non-standard addressing creates confusion on multi-agency channels.
</Warning>

## Callsign rules summary

<Note>
  The following rules apply to all members on all channels at all times:

  - Always state your **full callsign** when initiating a transmission, including any division prefix.
  - **Do not share or borrow** another unit's callsign. Each callsign must map to exactly one person or apparatus at any given time.
  - If your callsign changes mid-shift (e.g. you take command of an incident), **advise dispatch** before using the new identifier.
  - Fire units speak as the **apparatus**; law enforcement units speak as **themselves**.
  - When working a joint incident, **confirm callsigns with all agencies** on scene at the start of the operation.
</Note>
