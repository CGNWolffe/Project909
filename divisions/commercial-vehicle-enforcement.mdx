The Commercial Vehicle Enforcement unit (CVE) is a specialized traffic unit responsible for keeping commercial trucks, buses, and freight traffic safe and compliant across San Bernardino County. The county sits on some of the busiest freight corridors in the state — the I-15 through the Cajon Pass, the I-10 through the Inland Empire, and long desert stretches toward Barstow and Needles. As a CVE deputy, you work stops and inspections that general patrol is not equipped to handle: equipment violations, overweight loads, unsecured cargo, hazardous materials, and driver hours-of-service.

## Division Overview

CVE deputies focus on commercial motor vehicles (CMVs) — any truck, tractor-trailer, bus, or vehicle carrying passengers or freight for commercial purposes. Your job is to identify unsafe commercial vehicles, conduct roadside inspections, place dangerous vehicles or drivers out of service, and support collision response when a commercial vehicle is involved. CVE works closely with the Highway Safety Division and coordinates with CHP on incidents along state highways.

## Responsibilities

- Conduct traffic stops and roadside inspections on commercial motor vehicles
- Run weigh station and checkpoint operations at approved locations
- Inspect equipment, cargo securement, and driver documentation
- Enforce weight, size, and load restrictions on county roads and highways
- Identify and manage hazardous materials (HAZMAT) loads during stops and collisions
- Issue out-of-service orders for unsafe vehicles or drivers
- Respond to and manage scenes involving commercial vehicle collisions or spilled loads
- Complete detailed inspection and enforcement reports for every CVE contact

## Qualifications

- Must be a **Constable** in good standing and authorized by CVE leadership before operating as a CVE unit
- Must show consistent, realistic traffic enforcement RP on general patrol
- Must understand CVE inspection procedures and out-of-service criteria before working solo
- Supervisor approval required for weigh station and checkpoint operations

## Operations

### Commercial Vehicle Stops

Commercial vehicles are large, heavy, and slow to stop. Plan your stop before you light them up.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a safe location">
    Choose a wide shoulder, turnout, truck stop, or off-ramp with room for the full length of the vehicle. Avoid curves, hills, bridges, and narrow shoulders.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Call it out">
    Advise Dispatch of your location, the vehicle (truck, trailer, or bus), the company name or DOT number if visible, and the plate. Note if the vehicle shows HAZMAT placards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Position your unit">
    Park offset behind the trailer so you are visible to traffic and clear of the truck's blind spots. Keep emergency lighting on for the duration of the stop.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make contact">
    Approach on the passenger side when traffic allows. Ask the driver to set the parking brake and shut off the engine before you begin.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect documents">
    Request the driver's commercial driver license (CDL), medical card, registration, proof of insurance, bill of lading or shipping papers, and logbook or ELD record.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Roadside Inspections

CVE uses a tiered inspection system. Choose the level that fits the stop and log it in your report.

| Level | Name | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Level I** | Full Inspection | Driver documents plus a full walk-around and under-vehicle check of brakes, tires, lights, coupling, frame, and cargo securement |
| **Level II** | Walk-Around | Driver documents and a walk-around check of everything visible without going under the vehicle |
| **Level III** | Driver Only | CDL, medical card, hours of service, and shipping papers — no vehicle check |
| **Level IV** | Special | A one-time check of a single item, such as a specific equipment complaint |

<Note>
  Level I inspections take time. Request a second unit to manage traffic if you are working a full inspection on a busy highway.
</Note>

### Weight and Size Enforcement

- Commercial vehicles may be directed to an open weigh station or a portable scale site set up by CVE.
- Common violations include overweight axles, gross weight over the legal limit, and oversized loads running without a permit or escort.
- Oversized or overweight loads must carry a valid permit and follow the approved route. A load off its permitted route is a violation.
- Trucks found significantly overweight may be ordered to stay at the scale until the load is reduced or redistributed.

### Hazardous Materials

HAZMAT loads are identified by diamond placards on the vehicle and by the shipping papers.

- Note the placard number and class on the radio as soon as you see it.
- Do not let smoking or open flame near the vehicle during the stop.
- If the load is leaking, damaged, or involved in a collision, stand back upwind and uphill, request the **Fire Department and a HAZMAT response**, and close the area.
- Do not attempt to move, open, or clean up a hazardous load yourself.

<Warning>
  Treat any unknown leak or spill from a commercial vehicle as hazardous until Fire confirms otherwise.
</Warning>

### Out-of-Service Orders

An out-of-service (OOS) order means the vehicle or driver may not continue until the problem is fixed. Issue an OOS order when you find:

- **Vehicle:** brakes out of adjustment or not working, flat or badly damaged tires, broken coupling devices, unsecured or shifting cargo, or missing required lighting at night
- **Driver:** no valid CDL, expired or missing medical card, over the legal hours of service, or signs of impairment

When you issue an OOS order, the vehicle stays parked until it is repaired, towed, or a qualified replacement driver arrives. Document the reason and the outcome in your report.

### Commercial Vehicle Collisions

- Secure the scene and request additional units for traffic control — commercial collisions often block multiple lanes.
- Check for HAZMAT placards before approaching.
- Request Fire and EMS for injuries, fuel leaks, or spilled loads, and a heavy-duty tow for the truck and trailer.
- Once the scene is safe, complete a post-collision inspection of the commercial vehicle to identify equipment failures.

## Rules and Restrictions

- **CVE vehicles are restricted to CVE members only.**
- **Only stop commercial vehicles at safe locations.** Never stop a loaded truck where it blocks traffic or cannot pull fully off the road.
- **Every inspection must have a reason.** Stops and inspections must be based on a violation, an equipment issue, or an approved checkpoint operation.
- **Weigh station and checkpoint operations require supervisor approval** before they start.
- **Do not handle HAZMAT loads yourself.** Request Fire and follow their direction.
- Document every CVE stop, inspection, and OOS order in your report.

<Warning>
  Unauthorized use of CVE vehicles, unsafe stops, or inspections without a lawful reason may result in disciplinary action or **removal from CVE**.
</Warning>

## How to Apply

Speak with a CVE supervisor or command member in-game or through the community Discord to express interest in joining Commercial Vehicle Enforcement. Show steady patrol activity, realistic traffic stops, and an understanding of commercial vehicle safety before you apply.

<Tip>
  Strong traffic enforcement on general patrol is the best preparation for CVE. Leadership looks for deputies who already handle stops safely and write clear reports.
</Tip>
