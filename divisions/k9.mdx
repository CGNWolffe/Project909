The K9 Division pairs certified handlers with trained police canines to support suspect tracking, area and building searches, and high-risk operations across San Bernardino County. A K9 team is a specialist resource, not a patrol tool. The handler is accountable for every deployment, every bite, and every moment the dog is working.

<Info>
  This page is a Project 909 roleplay SOP adapted from standard California law enforcement canine practice. It is not a copy of any real agency's policy. Where it overlaps with [Use of Force](/procedures/use-of-force), the stricter rule applies.
</Info>

## Purpose and authority

K9 deployment is a use of force. Treat it with the same gravity as any other force option, and expect the same scrutiny.

- You may deploy your canine only when the deployment is lawful, justified, and proportionate to the threat and the offense.
- You are in command of your dog. Nobody else gives the dog commands, and nobody else may handle it.
- A supervisor's approval authorizes a deployment. It never transfers your responsibility for it.

## Handler selection and certification

<Steps>
  <Step title="Meet the prerequisites">
    You must be a **Deputy Sheriff** in good standing, past field training, with solid patrol fundamentals and report writing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply to K9 leadership">
    Speak with a K9 supervisor or command member in-game or through the community Discord. Show that you understand use-of-force principles and, just as important, restraint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete K9 training">
    Training covers handler control, deployment criteria, announcements, call-off discipline, search procedure, and K9 reporting. K9 leadership signs you off as a **certified K9 handler**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stay current">
    Certification is ongoing. Handlers who are inactive, or who show poor control or judgment, may be re-evaluated or removed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Membership in the K9 Division is separate from rank. Deputies keep their patrol rank while assigned (see [Ranks](/sbcsd/ranks)).

## Responsibilities

- Track fleeing or hiding suspects after a foot pursuit or from a known last-seen location
- Search buildings, yards, and open areas, and recover evidence
- Support ERT and patrol on high-risk incidents that need a K9 capability
- Keep positive control of your canine at all times
- Report every deployment, including ones that ended without a bite
- Keep the K9 vehicle and equipment in service condition

## Deployment

### When K9 is appropriate

| Use | Appropriate when |
| --- | --- |
| **Tracking** | A suspect has fled on foot from a crime or lawful stop and you have a last-seen point or scent trail |
| **Building or area search** | A suspect is believed to be hiding and a search by officers alone would put them at unreasonable risk |
| **Apprehension (bite)** | The suspect is wanted for a serious offense or poses a threat, is refusing to surrender, and lesser options have failed or are not safe |
| **Evidence recovery** | Property or evidence was discarded during a flight or incident |

### When K9 is not appropriate

- The suspect is compliant, surrendering, restrained, or in custody
- The offense is minor and the suspect is not a danger to anyone
- The suspect's location is unknown and no search area has been contained
- Bystanders, children, or other officers are in the area and cannot be cleared
- You cannot keep control of your dog

<Warning>
  A deployment that fails these tests is an unjustified use of force, even if it ends without a bite.
</Warning>

### Request and authorization

<Steps>
  <Step title="Request K9 through dispatch">
    The requesting unit gives dispatch the offense, suspect description, last seen location, direction of travel, and any known weapons.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Brief on arrival">
    The first unit on scene briefs you on what happened, where the suspect was last seen, and who and what is inside the area.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contain the area">
    Patrol sets a perimeter. Only officers needed for the search stay in the area. Everyone else holds the perimeter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get supervisor approval">
    Before any search or apprehension deployment, obtain **explicit supervisor approval** and log it in Imperial CAD. If no supervisor is available, notify dispatch and hold until one responds, unless delay would put someone in immediate danger.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Announcement and release

<Steps>
  <Step title="Announce">
    Whenever it is safe, give a loud, clear warning that identifies the agency, orders the suspect to surrender, and states that a police dog will be released and may bite.

    ```text
    "Sheriff's Department K9! Come out now and show me your hands.
    If you do not surrender, I will release a police dog and you may be bitten!"
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow time to comply">
    Pause long enough for the suspect to hear and respond. For a building or large area, repeat the announcement from more than one position.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Release or hold">
    Release the dog only if the suspect still refuses and the deployment is still justified. If the suspect surrenders or the situation changes, hold the dog and reassess.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You may skip the announcement only when giving it would put you or another person in immediate danger. If you skip it, say so on the radio and explain why in your report.

## Apprehension and call-off

- **Keep the dog under command.** Hold it ready to recall at all times.
- **Call the dog off the moment the suspect stops resisting.** That means on the ground, showing hands, or otherwise compliant. Never let the dog keep biting a subject who has complied.
- **Secure the dog first.** Put it on lead or back in the vehicle before officers move in to handcuff.
- **Use cover officers.** They handle the arrest while you control the dog.

<Note>
  If the dog does not release on command, get control of the dog, keep other officers clear, and report the failed call-off to your supervisor immediately.
</Note>

## Medical aid

<Steps>
  <Step title="Request EMS">
    Request EMS for **every bite or injury**, however minor, on the radio as soon as the suspect is secured.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Render aid">
    Give first aid until EMS takes over. Do not leave an injured suspect unattended.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document the injury">
    Record the injury location, aid given, and EMS response. A supervisor documents and photographs the injury where appropriate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If the dog is injured, treat it and transport it to a veterinary hospital. If the dog may have been exposed to opioids, naloxone may be given to the dog and it must be taken for veterinary treatment.

## Reporting and review

Write a report for every K9 deployment, whether or not the dog bit anyone.

| Include | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Reason** | The offense and why K9 was the right tool |
| **Authorization** | The supervisor who approved it and the CAD log entry |
| **Announcement** | What was said, from where, and how long you waited, or why none was given |
| **Outcome** | What the dog did, for how long, and when you called it off |
| **Injuries and medical** | Suspect, officer, or dog injuries, and the EMS response |

A supervisor reviews every bite against this SOP and [Use of Force](/procedures/use-of-force). Unjustified or poorly controlled deployments go to K9 leadership for review.

## Equipment and vehicle

<Card title="2024 Chevrolet Tahoe — K9 Unit" icon="truck">
  A marked K9 Tahoe with a rear kennel, heat alarm and climate control for the dog's safety, and storage for handler equipment. **Restricted to certified K9 handlers.** No one else may drive or borrow it without K9 or department command authorization.
</Card>

- Never leave the dog in a vehicle without working climate control and a heat alarm.
- A K9 vehicle is the one exception to the rule against leaving a department vehicle running unattended, when the dog is inside.
- Keep the dog on lead around the public unless it is actively deployed.

## Rules and restrictions

- **K9 vehicles are for certified handlers only.**
- **Every deployment must be lawful, justified, and approved.** Unjustified deployment is a use-of-force violation.
- **You control your dog at all times.** If it causes unnecessary harm, the responsibility is yours.
- **Never release on a compliant or surrendering subject.**
- **Announce before release** unless it endangers someone, and document why if you skip it.
- **Request EMS for every bite.**
- **Report every deployment.**

<Warning>
  Unauthorized K9 use, improper deployment, or failure to maintain the correct uniform may result in **immediate removal from the K9 Unit**. K9 capability is a privilege, not a right.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  K9 leadership wants handlers who understand restraint as much as deployment. Show that you know _when not_ to use K9. That matters as much as knowing when to deploy.
</Tip>
