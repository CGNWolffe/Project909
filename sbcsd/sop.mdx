These standard operating procedures apply to every SBCSD member — deputies, sergeants, and command staff — for the duration of a scheduled patrol shift. They're modeled after real-world sheriff's department policy manuals to keep roleplay grounded and consistent. For step-by-step operational guides, see [Patrol Procedures](/procedures/patrol), [Report Writing](/procedures/report-writing), and [Use of Force](/procedures/use-of-force).

## Chain of Command

The Department operates on a strict chain of command. Members are expected to know their immediate supervisor and follow the proper reporting structure at all times.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Deputy">
    Handles patrol, calls for service, traffic enforcement, and initial incident response.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Corporal / Senior Deputy">
    Field-training and mentoring newer deputies; may act as a shift lead in a sergeant's absence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sergeant">
    Supervises a patrol squad, approves reports, and is the first point of contact for escalated incidents.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lieutenant / Captain">
    Oversees station operations, staffing, and policy enforcement.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Orders should flow down this chain. Deputies who disagree with a supervisor's on-scene direction should comply first and address concerns afterward through the proper channel — not on scene, and never over open radio traffic.

## Shift Procedures

### Sign-On

1. Log on to Imperial CAD with your correct callsign and station.
2. Conduct a radio check with Dispatch before going 10-8 (in service).
3. Inspect your assigned vehicle — lights, sirens, first aid kit, and MDT functionality.
4. Verify your duty weapon, less-lethal options, and body armor are present and serviceable.

### During Shift

- Maintain an active CAD status at all times (10-8, 10-6, 10-7, etc.) so Dispatch and other units know your availability.
- Advise Dispatch before self-initiating a stop, and provide location, plate, and occupant count when able.
- Request backup for anything that reasonably escalates risk — don't wait until a situation is already out of hand.
- Complete reports before your shift ends whenever possible; see [Report Writing](/procedures/report-writing).

### Sign-Off

1. Clear all open calls or hand them off to an oncoming unit.
2. Return your vehicle and equipment to standard.
3. Log off CAD and advise Dispatch you are 10-42 (end of shift).

## Conduct Standards

- Members must remain in character and maintain a professional, respectful tone with the public and with other units, even during high-stress scenarios.
- Excessive force, unnecessary escalation, or metagaming information a character wouldn't reasonably know is prohibited.
- Members must follow real-world legal procedure for detentions, searches, and arrests as outlined in [Use of Force](/procedures/use-of-force).
- Off-duty conduct that reflects poorly on the Department or the community may still be subject to review.

## Performance and Discipline

- New deputies are evaluated by their FTO throughout field training before being cleared for solo patrol.
- Supervisors may issue verbal corrections, written warnings, or refer a member to Internal Affairs for serious or repeated violations.
- Disciplinary outcomes range from retraining and suspension to termination, depending on severity.
- Members under investigation are expected to cooperate fully and truthfully with Internal Affairs.
