Dispatch assigns apparatus to a call based on the reported call type and the information available at the time. The assignments below are the department default — they cover the majority of calls as they are initially reported. Command can request additional resources at any time, and dispatch will start them without waiting for on-scene confirmation if the initial report warrants it. Know what your unit is expected to bring before you go en route.

## Response Assignments

| Call Type | Default Response |
| --------- | ---------------- |
| Structure fire | Two engines, one ladder, one medic, battalion chief |
| Vehicle fire | One engine |
| Motor vehicle collision | One engine, one rescue, one medic |
| Medical call | One medic |
| Alarm activation | One engine |
| Brush or outdoor fire | One engine, one tanker |

<Warning>
A reported entrapment, a report of people trapped inside, or a caller confirming a working fire upgrades the response immediately. Do not wait until arrival to confirm — start the additional apparatus now and cancel them if they are not needed. A cancelled unit costs nothing; a delayed unit at a working fire can cost lives.
</Warning>

## On-Arrival Procedures

Every unit that arrives on scene follows the same sequence. The first-arriving officer drives this sequence; all other arriving units listen and slot into the assignments they are given.

<Steps>

<Step title="Give a size-up">
The first unit on scene broadcasts a size-up before stepping off the apparatus. Include the building type, what you can see from the exterior, and whether any occupants are reported inside or visible. "Engine One on scene — two-storey residential, smoke showing from the Alpha-Bravo corner, one occupant reported inside." Every unit still en route is listening to this transmission and beginning to plan their assignment.
</Step>

<Step title="Establish command">
Immediately after the size-up, the first-arriving officer establishes command and announces their command post location using the Alpha-Bravo-Charlie-Delta side convention. "Engine One has command, Alpha side." From this moment, all tactical decisions on scene flow through command.
</Step>

<Step title="Assign incoming units before they arrive">
Give each apparatus arriving after you a task before they reach the scene. "Ladder Two, take the roof." "Rescue One, stand by for extrication." "Medic Two, stage at the end of the block." Unassigned crews default to freelancing, which creates accountability gaps and hazards. If you do not yet have a task for a unit, hold them at a staging location and assign them as soon as you do.
</Step>

<Step title="Account for all personnel">
Maintain a running count of who is inside the structure, in what area, and doing what. Run an explicit headcount every time your tactical strategy changes — when you move from offensive to defensive, when you open a new division, or when you reassign a crew. If you cannot account for a member, that is an immediate priority-one emergency.
</Step>

<Step title="Terminate the incident and report">
When the scene is controlled and all hazards are mitigated, announce the scene clear over the air and return all assigned units to available status through dispatch. Before you sign off, file the incident report. No unit signs off for the shift with an open, unreported incident.
</Step>

</Steps>

## Scene Coordination with Law Enforcement

Shared scenes with SBCSD and CHP are common. The division of responsibility is fixed — know it before you arrive so there is no confusion on scene.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Traffic control and scene perimeter belong to police" icon="triangle-alert">
Road closures, traffic diversions, and perimeter establishment are law enforcement responsibilities. Fire apparatus can be positioned for crew protection, but you do not set a road block, direct civilian traffic, or establish a perimeter independently. If you need a road closed or a perimeter expanded, request it through dispatch and let law enforcement action it. Apparatus positioning for protection from traffic is your call — road closure is not.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Fire scenes that become criminal investigations" icon="clipboard-list">
If a fire scene shows signs of being suspicious — unusual burn patterns, accelerant odours, prior threat reports — limit your overhaul to what is strictly required for safety and notify dispatch that the scene may be investigative. Do not move debris, clear materials, or allow unnecessary foot traffic through areas that have not been examined. Hand the scene to law enforcement as intact as fire suppression allows. Document what you did and in what area before you clear.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="One channel for the incident" icon="radio">
Command communicates with dispatch. Individual crews on scene communicate with command. Fire units do not transmit directly to police units on a separate channel during a shared incident — route any coordination through your incident commander, who relays to the law enforcement commander as needed. A single command channel prevents contradictory orders and keeps everyone's radio picture consistent.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
