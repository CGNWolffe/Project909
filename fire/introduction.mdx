The Project 909 Fire Department responds to structure fires, vehicle fires, medical calls, motor vehicle collisions, and technical rescues across the San Bernardino County response area. Members regularly share scenes with SBCSD and CHP units, so strong radio discipline and a clear understanding of command structure matter just as much as fireground skill. Read this page in full before your first shift, then follow the links at the bottom to apparatus and response procedures.

## Ranks

Every member holds a rank that defines their authority on scene and their supervisory responsibilities. Probationary members must work under direct supervision at all times — this is a safety requirement, not a formality.

| Rank | Role |
| ---- | ---- |
| Probationary Firefighter | New member under direct supervision; cannot ride alone or act as crew lead |
| Firefighter | Full member cleared for independent assignment on all standard calls |
| Lieutenant | Leads a single apparatus and its crew; responsible for crew accountability |
| Captain | Commands a station and supervises multiple crews across shifts |
| Chief | Overall command authority for the department; sets policy and procedure |

## Member Expectations

These expectations apply on every shift, on every call, regardless of call type or who else is on scene.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Sign on and off with dispatch at every shift" icon="radio">
Your shift starts when you contact dispatch and ends when you advise dispatch you are clear. Nobody rides unassigned. Dispatch cannot send you to a call or account for you on scene if it does not know you are in service. Sign on when you spawn in and sign off before you log out — no exceptions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Scene safety comes before the task" icon="hard-hat">
Full personal protective equipment goes on before you leave the apparatus on any working call. You do not enter a structure alone under any circumstances. If a task requires a skill or equipment level you have not been trained on, you hold at your assigned position and advise command. Operating outside your training level puts you and your crew at risk.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Command is single and clear" icon="users">
The first officer to arrive establishes command and holds it until it is formally transferred to a higher-ranking officer. When command transfers, the outgoing commander announces it over the air — "Command transfers to Battalion One" — so that all units and dispatch know who is in charge. There is never more than one incident commander on a scene at a time.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Work with police, not around them" icon="shield">
On shared scenes, SBCSD and CHP own traffic control and the perimeter. Request a road block through dispatch rather than positioning apparatus to do the job yourself. Coordinate with law enforcement through your incident commander, not by freelancing on their channel. Fire handles the hazard; police handle the scene boundary.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
