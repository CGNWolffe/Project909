Getting started in Project 909 is straightforward when you follow the steps in order. This checklist walks you through everything you need to complete before hitting the road for your first patrol. Work through each step carefully — skipping ahead often creates problems during your Field Training Officer evaluation.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Join the Discord Server">
    The Discord server is the community's primary hub for announcements, department communications, scheduling, and staff contact. Everything flows through Discord, so joining is the first thing you need to do.

    Once you are in the server, read through the welcome and information channels before posting or asking questions. Most common questions are already answered there.

    <Note>
      Make sure your Discord display name matches the name you will use in-game. Mismatched names can delay your onboarding and CAD setup.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read the Rules and Expectations">
    Before you do anything else in the community, read the full rules and expectations document. Ignorance of the rules is not accepted as an excuse for violations — supervisors and staff expect every member to know the standards from day one.

    Pay close attention to the sections on conduct, radio discipline, and activity requirements. Specialized units have stricter minimums that you will need to plan around.

    <Card title="Rules and Expectations" icon="scale" href="/rules-and-expectations">
      Read the full community rules before your first patrol or FTO session.
    </Card>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Pick Your Department and Role">
    Project 909 has three departments to choose from: the San Bernardino County Sheriff's Department, the California Highway Patrol, and the Fire Department. Review the introduction page to understand the focus and responsibilities of each before you commit.

    Once you select a department, you will be assigned your starting rank and placed in the appropriate Discord channels and CAD access group. You can express interest in specialized divisions later — new members begin on standard patrol first.

    <Note>
      You must complete your FTO training in the department you selected. Switching departments after FTO requires a formal request and may require repeating portions of training.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Complete FTO Supervised Patrol">
    Field Training Officer (FTO) supervised patrol is a mandatory requirement for all new members. During your FTO sessions, a certified training officer will observe your patrol conduct, radio usage, traffic stop technique, and overall professionalism.

    You must pass your FTO evaluation before you are cleared for independent patrol. Treat every FTO session as a performance review — be on time, in the correct uniform, and ready to communicate clearly.

    <Tip>
      Ask your FTO questions during quieter moments on patrol. They are there to help you succeed. Showing initiative and asking good questions leaves a strong impression.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Learn Radio Codes and Phonetics">
    Clear, professional radio communication is a core skill for every Project 909 member. Before your FTO sessions begin, familiarise yourself with the ten-codes and NATO phonetic alphabet used on the server. Poor radio discipline is one of the most common reasons new members struggle during evaluation.

    <Card title="Radio Usage Guide" icon="radio" href="/communication/radio-usage">
      Learn proper radio etiquette, callsign structure, and communication standards.
    </Card>

    <Card title="Ten-Codes Reference" icon="hash" href="/communication/ten-codes">
      Full reference list of ten-codes used on Project 909.
    </Card>

    <Note>
      Practice your radio transmissions before going on patrol. A clear, confident radio voice makes a noticeable difference during your FTO evaluation.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Get Familiar with Imperial CAD">
    Imperial CAD is the computer-aided dispatch system used by all Project 909 members for report writing, unit status updates, warrant checks, and incident logging. You will need to create an account and link it to your in-game identity before your first patrol.

    Once you have access, navigate through the CAD interface and locate the report writing section. Read the report writing guide so you understand the expected format before your first call.

    <Card title="Report Writing Guide" icon="file-text" href="/procedures/report-writing">
      Learn how to write professional, detailed police reports in Imperial CAD.
    </Card>

    <Tip>
      Write your reports during downtime on patrol, not after you have logged off. Fresh details make for more accurate and professional reports.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## You Are Ready to Patrol

Once you have completed every step above, notify your supervisor or FTO coordinator in Discord. They will confirm your status and schedule your first supervised patrol. From there, your rank progression begins based on your performance, activity, and conduct on the server.

<Note>
  If you get stuck at any step, ask for help in the appropriate Discord channel. The community has experienced members who are happy to point you in the right direction.
</Note>
