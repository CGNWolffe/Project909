The California Highway Patrol is the state traffic and highway law enforcement agency within Project 909. Officers patrol the county's freeways and state highways — the I-15 through the Cajon Pass, the I-10 through the Inland Empire, and the open desert routes toward Barstow and Needles — with a focus on traffic safety, enforcement, and collision response.

## What Officers Do

Day-to-day CHP patrol covers:

- **Highway and freeway patrol** — proactive enforcement and visible presence on state routes
- **Traffic enforcement** — stops, citations, speed enforcement, and equipment violations
- **DUI enforcement** — detection, field sobriety testing, and arrest of impaired drivers
- **Collision investigation** — scene management, investigation, and reports for crashes on state highways
- **Pursuits** — vehicle pursuits under the department's pursuit policy
- **Motorist services** — disabled vehicles, road hazards, and traffic breaks

All units operate through Dispatch using Imperial CAD and the community radio system. CHP patrol units use the `Charlie` callsign prefix; supervisors use `Metro`. See [Callsigns](/communication/callsigns).

## Jurisdiction

CHP has primary responsibility for state highways, freeways, and unincorporated county roads for traffic matters. SBCSD handles general law enforcement in the county. When a call crosses lines — for example, a freeway pursuit that exits into a city — coordinate through Dispatch and hand off or share the scene as needed.

## Expectations

Officers are held to the community's standards of realism and professionalism at all times while on duty. In practice that means:

- Strong knowledge of traffic law and the vehicle code
- Following real-world procedure for stops, DUI investigations, and collision scenes
- Maintaining proper radio discipline and CAD status
- Respecting the chain of command
- Staying in character for the duration of a patrol

## Getting Started

New officers complete field training with an FTO as a Cadet before patrolling solo.
