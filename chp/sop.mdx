These standard operating procedures apply to every CHP member — cadets, officers, sergeants, and command staff — for the duration of a patrol shift. They're modeled after real-world highway patrol policy to keep roleplay grounded and consistent. For step-by-step operational guides, see [Patrol Procedures](/procedures/patrol), [Report Writing](/procedures/report-writing), and [Use of Force](/procedures/use-of-force).

## Chain of Command

<Steps>
  <Step title="Cadet">
    Patrols only with an assigned FTO. Learns stops, DUI investigations, collision scenes, and radio procedure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Officer">
    Handles highway patrol, traffic enforcement, DUI enforcement, and collision response.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sergeant">
    Supervises the shift, approves reports and pursuits, and is the first point of contact for escalated incidents.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lieutenant and above">
    Oversees area operations, staffing, divisions, and policy enforcement.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Orders flow down this chain. If you disagree with a supervisor's on-scene direction, comply first and raise it afterward through the proper channel — never on scene or over open radio.

## Shift Procedures

### Sign-On

1. Log on to Imperial CAD with your correct `Charlie` callsign (or `Metro` for supervisors).
2. Conduct a radio check with Dispatch before going 10-8.
3. Inspect your assigned vehicle — lights, siren, radar/lidar, flares, cones, first aid kit, and MDT.
4. Verify your duty weapon, less-lethal options, and body armor are present and serviceable.

### During Shift

- Keep your CAD status current at all times so Dispatch and other units know your availability.
- Call out every stop before you make contact: location (route, direction, and nearest exit or mile marker), plate, vehicle description, and occupant count.
- Request backup for anything that reasonably raises risk — felony stops, multiple occupants, or uncooperative drivers.
- Complete reports before the end of your shift whenever possible; see [Report Writing](/procedures/report-writing).

### Sign-Off

1. Clear all open calls or hand them off to an oncoming unit.
2. Return your vehicle and equipment to standard.
3. Log off CAD and advise Dispatch you are 10-42.

## Traffic Stops

- Choose a safe location: a wide shoulder, off-ramp, or surface street. Avoid stopping on curves, bridges, or in construction zones.
- On freeways, position your unit offset to the left of the violator to create a safety lane, and use a **passenger-side approach** whenever traffic allows.
- Keep emergency lighting on for the full duration of the stop.
- Every stop needs a lawful reason — a moving violation, equipment violation, or reasonable suspicion of a crime.

## DUI Enforcement

1. Note the driving behaviour that led to the stop.
2. Look for objective signs of impairment on contact (odor, speech, eyes, coordination).
3. Administer standardized field sobriety tests where it is safe to do so.
4. Offer a preliminary alcohol screening (PAS) test as appropriate.
5. Arrest when probable cause exists, and document every step in your report.

## Pursuit Policy

- A pursuit may only begin when a driver clearly refuses to stop and the need to apprehend outweighs the danger to the public.
- Advise Dispatch immediately: direction of travel, speed, vehicle description, reason for the pursuit, and traffic conditions.
- No more than **two units** actively pursue unless a supervisor authorizes more. Air Operations takes the lead when available.
- **PIT maneuvers** and other intervention tactics require supervisor approval and must only be used at speeds and locations where they are reasonable.
- A supervisor may terminate a pursuit at any time. When told to terminate, disengage, turn off emergency equipment, and advise Dispatch.

<Warning>
  Pursuing through heavy traffic, residential areas, or at unsafe speeds without supervisor approval is a policy violation, regardless of the outcome.
</Warning>

## Collision Investigation

- Protect the scene first: position your unit to shield it, set flares or cones, and request traffic control if lanes are blocked.
- Request Fire and EMS for any injuries or hazards.
- Identify drivers and witnesses, document vehicle positions and damage, and determine the primary collision factor.
- Request a tow for disabled vehicles and clear lanes as soon as the investigation allows.

## Conduct Standards

- Stay in character and keep a professional, respectful tone with the public and with other units, even in high-stress scenarios.
- Excessive force, unnecessary escalation, and metagaming are prohibited.
- Follow real-world legal procedure for detentions, searches, and arrests as outlined in [Use of Force](/procedures/use-of-force).
- Off-duty conduct that reflects poorly on the department or the community may still be subject to review.

## Performance and Discipline

- Cadets are evaluated by their FTO throughout field training before being cleared for solo patrol.
- Supervisors may issue verbal corrections, written warnings, or refer a member to Internal Affairs for serious or repeated violations.
- Outcomes range from retraining and suspension to termination, depending on severity.
- Members under investigation must cooperate fully and truthfully with Internal Affairs.
