The use of force is the most consequential decision you will make on patrol. In the Project 909 roleplay community, realistic and proportionate force application is expected at all times — it produces better roleplay, protects other players' experience, and reflects the professional standards that SBCSD and CHP members represent. Force must always be the minimum necessary to achieve a lawful objective. Every application of force, from a firm voice command to the deployment of a firearm, must be justified by the circumstances at the time and documented in your report.

## Use-of-Force Continuum

The continuum describes a graduated escalation — and de-escalation — of force options available to you. Each level is only justified when lower levels have been attempted or are clearly insufficient given the threat.

| Level | Force Option | When Justified |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Verbal Commands** | All contacts. Always the starting point. Commands must be clear, direct, and audible. |
| 2 | **Verbal Warnings of Force** | Subject is non-compliant but not yet physically resistive. Warn before escalating. |
| 3 | **Physical Control / Hands-On** | Subject is actively resisting lawful commands. Use restraint techniques to gain compliance. |
| 4 | **Less-Lethal Force** | Subject is actively aggressive or poses a physical threat. Includes taser deployment and OC spray. |
| 5 | **K9 Deployment** | Subject is fleeing a serious offence, is hiding in a containment area, or poses a threat that K9 presence is likely to resolve. Supervisor authorisation required. |
| 6 | **Lethal Force** | Subject poses an imminent threat of death or grievous bodily harm to you or another person. No other option is available or reasonable in the time available. |

<Note>
  Every force escalation must be preceded by a verbal warning unless doing so would place you or another person in immediate danger. If you skip a level, your report must articulate exactly why lower options were insufficient or unavailable.
</Note>

## Verbal Commands and Physical Control

Verbal commands are your first and most frequently used tool. Even in high-tension situations, a clear, calm, authoritative voice is often enough to gain compliance.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Issue Clear and Direct Commands">
    State what you want the subject to do — do not ask. Speak loudly enough to be heard, keep your instructions simple, and give the subject a reasonable moment to comply before escalating.

    ```text
    "Stop! Do not move! Show me your hands!"
    "Driver, turn off the engine and place both hands on the steering wheel!"
    "Get on the ground — face down, hands behind your back!"
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Warn Before Escalating">
    If verbal commands are not producing compliance, announce the next level of force before you apply it. This gives the subject a final opportunity to comply and documents your escalation clearly for your report.

    ```text
    "Stop resisting or I will deploy my taser!"
    "Do not advance — I am authorized to use force!"
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply Only the Force Necessary">
    Hands-on control techniques should be the minimum required to overcome resistance. Once a subject is compliant and restrained, cease all application of force immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Less-Lethal Force

Less-lethal options — taser and OC spray — are appropriate when a subject is actively aggressive, physically resistant, or poses a threat that verbal commands have failed to resolve. They are not appropriate against passive non-compliance.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Announce Your Intent">
    Before deploying a taser or OC spray, issue a verbal warning. Give the subject one final opportunity to comply.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deploy and Reassess">
    Apply the tool, then immediately reassess the threat level. If the subject is compliant, transition to physical control and restrain them. Do not continue to apply less-lethal force once the subject is down and compliant.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document and Report">
    Every deployment of a less-lethal tool — whether it achieves compliance or not — must be documented in your report. Include the reason for deployment, the response, and any injuries.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  In roleplay, announcing taser deployment before you fire creates better RP for everyone involved. Give the other player a moment to respond to the verbal warning before you follow through.
</Tip>

## K9 Deployment

K9 units are a specialist resource. Deployment must be proportionate, justified, and authorised by a supervisor before the K9 is released on a subject.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm Supervisor Authorisation">
    Request K9 through dispatch and obtain explicit supervisor approval before deploying your dog. Log the authorisation in Imperial CAD.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Issue a K9 Warning">
    Before releasing the K9, broadcast a loud and clear warning giving the subject one final opportunity to surrender.

    ```text
    "Sheriff's Office! Come out now or I will release the dog! 
    You have ten seconds to comply!"
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Release Only When Justified">
    K9 deployment is appropriate when a suspect is actively fleeing a felony stop, is concealed in a building or wooded area following a serious offence, or poses a physical threat that other less-lethal options have failed to resolve. Do not deploy K9 against a compliant or surrendering subject.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Secure the K9 on Compliance">
    The moment the subject is on the ground and ceasing resistance, call off the dog. Transition to physical restraint. A subject who is on the ground and compliant is no longer a justification for continued K9 engagement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request Medical if Required">
    Any K9 bite must be followed by a request for EMS to evaluate the subject. Document the deployment, the subject's behaviour, and the medical response in your report.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  K9 deployment in roleplay should reflect realistic standards. Using a K9 against a subject who is surrendering, already restrained, or posing no threat will be treated the same as excessive force and may result in a personnel review.
</Note>

## ERT Tactical Deployment

The Emergency Response Team (ERT) is activated for high-risk tactical situations that exceed the capacity of patrol units. ERT resources — including the Bearcat armoured vehicle — require command-level authorisation.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the Threshold Situation">
    ERT activation is appropriate for: barricaded armed suspects, hostage situations, high-risk warrant service where armed resistance is anticipated, and active shooter incidents where patrol containment has been established.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request Through Chain of Command">
    Patrol supervisors submit an ERT request through the duty commander or Sheriff's Operations. CHP officers route requests through their Area Commander. Do not self-deploy ERT assets.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Establish a Perimeter Before ERT Arrival">
    Patrol units maintain the outer perimeter and keep civilians clear of the zone. Do not attempt entry or tactical action until ERT assumes command of the inner perimeter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transfer Command on ERT Arrival">
    When ERT arrives, the first-on-scene patrol supervisor briefs the ERT Commander and formally transfers tactical command. Patrol units shift to perimeter support and traffic control roles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bearcat Deployment Requires Command Approval">
    The Bearcat is authorised only when there is a confirmed or highly probable armed threat that poses a direct risk to officers or the public. Its deployment requires sign-off from the ERT Commander and duty command. Patrol supervisors do not have independent authority to deploy the Bearcat.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Firearm Use — Rules of Engagement

Lethal force is the most irreversible action available to you. In both real law enforcement and serious roleplay, firearm use demands the highest standard of justification and the clearest possible articulation in your report.

| Situation | Permitted? | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| Subject poses imminent lethal threat to you | ✅ Yes | Must be immediate, not speculative |
| Subject poses imminent lethal threat to a third party | ✅ Yes | You must be in a position to intervene safely |
| Armed suspect fleeing on foot, no ongoing threat | ❌ No | Flight alone does not justify lethal force |
| Unarmed subject in a physical altercation | ❌ No | Escalate to less-lethal; reassess continuously |
| Vehicle as a weapon — subject driving at you | ✅ Conditional | Move clear if possible; lethal force only if escape is not feasible |
| Warning shot | ❌ No | Not authorised under SBCSD or CHP policy |

<Warning>
  **The following are absolute prohibitions and will result in immediate personnel review or removal:**

  - Random or retaliatory shootings with no imminent threat justification (RDM — Random Deathmatch)
  - Discharging a firearm into a crowd
  - Shooting a fleeing suspect in the back who poses no ongoing lethal threat
  - Firing a warning shot
  - Using lethal force against a compliant or surrendering subject
  - Discharging a weapon for any reason not documented and justified in a post-incident report

  These actions violate both Project 909 community rules and the realistic law enforcement standards this community is built on.
</Warning>

<Note>
  In roleplay, **always give the other player a moment to react** before firing. Issue verbal commands, announce your weapon, and allow a reasonable reaction window. Immediate draws and instant shots without any RP escalation are poor form and are treated as rule violations. Good use-of-force roleplay makes the scene better for everyone involved.
</Note>
