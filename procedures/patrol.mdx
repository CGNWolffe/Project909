Effective patrol is the foundation of every shift. Whether you are riding solo or paired with a partner, following a consistent routine from sign-on to sign-off keeps you, your fellow deputies and officers, and the public safe. This page walks through the standard patrol workflow used by San Bernardino County Sheriff's Department (SBCSD) and California Highway Patrol (CHP) members in the Project 909 community, and covers the three most common patrol situations you will encounter: traffic stops, vehicle pursuits, and scene management.

## Patrol Workflow

Every shift follows the same basic sequence regardless of your unit or assignment.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign On with Dispatch">
    Before you begin patrol, radio dispatch with your unit number, assigned zone or beat, and status. Confirm you have received any active calls or be-on-the-lookout (BOLO) advisories before clearing.

    ```text
    "Dispatch, Unit 4-Lincoln-12 is 10-8 in Zone 3, no outstanding calls."
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Conduct Active Patrol">
    Drive your assigned zone with intention. Vary your route to maintain visibility. Observe traffic patterns, known problem locations, and any suspicious activity. Log your patrol area in Imperial CAD so supervisors and dispatch know your position at all times.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Respond to Calls">
    When dispatch assigns a call, acknowledge immediately with your unit number and an ETA. Update dispatch if your status changes en route — code upgrade, back-up requested, or secondary unit needed. Refer to [Ten-Codes](/communication/ten-codes) for correct radio shorthand on all transmissions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear and Document">
    Once a call is resolved, clear the scene over radio, update Imperial CAD with the outcome, and complete any required reports before going back 10-8. Do not clear a scene that still requires a supervisor or investigative presence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign Off with Dispatch">
    At end of shift, advise dispatch you are 10-7, confirm all open reports are filed in Imperial CAD, and transfer any unresolved calls to the oncoming unit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Traffic Stops, Pursuits, and Scene Management

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Traffic Stops">
    Traffic stops are among the most frequent — and most unpredictable — encounters you will have on patrol. Following a consistent approach every time reduces risk and produces better report documentation.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Observe and Confirm the Violation">
        Identify the violation clearly before activating emergency equipment. Note the vehicle description (make, model, colour, and plate), direction of travel, and exact location. Announce the stop over radio before activating lights.

        ```text
        "Dispatch, Unit 4-Lincoln-12 initiating a traffic stop on a black Dodge Charger, 
        California plate 7-Adam-Frank-3, southbound Route 68 approaching Joshua Road."
        ```
      </Step>
      <Step title="Activate Emergency Equipment and Position">
        Activate your light bar and siren. Position your unit at an offset angle behind the vehicle — typically one lane to the left — to provide a safety buffer between yourself and passing traffic. Leave enough room to exit your door safely.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Approach and Make Contact">
        Approach from the driver side unless conditions dictate otherwise. Announce your agency and reason for the stop clearly. Request the driver's licence, registration, and proof of insurance. Remain observant throughout — note demeanour, visible contraband, or signs of impairment.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Run the Vehicle and Occupants">
        Return to your unit to run the plate, driver's licence, and any warrants through Imperial CAD. Advise dispatch of the stop status and any flags that return.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Issue Citation or Effect Arrest">
        Based on your investigation, issue a written citation, a verbal warning, or place the subject under arrest. Document all enforcement actions. If arrest is required, call for a secondary unit before removing the subject from the vehicle.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Clear the Stop">
        Advise dispatch the stop is clear, update Imperial CAD, and complete your report promptly while the details are fresh.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Tip>
      Always announce your traffic stop location to dispatch **before** you exit your vehicle. If a stop goes sideways immediately, dispatch already has your location.
    </Tip>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Pursuits">
    Vehicle pursuits carry significant risk to the public, to you, and to other units. Initiate them only when the criteria below are met, and be prepared to terminate at any point when the risk outweighs the benefit.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Assess Before You Initiate">
        Before committing to a pursuit, rapidly evaluate: the severity of the known or suspected offence, current traffic and road conditions, time of day, and whether the suspect's identity is already known. A misdemeanour traffic violation alone rarely justifies a pursuit through a populated area.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Announce the Pursuit">
        As soon as a vehicle fails to yield and you are committed to following, broadcast on the primary channel.

        ```text
        "Dispatch, Unit 4-Lincoln-12, in pursuit of a black Dodge Charger, 
        California plate 7-Adam-Frank-3, northbound Interstate 15 at Exit 42 — 
        suspect failed to yield on a traffic stop. Speeds approximately 90 mph."
        ```
      </Step>
      <Step title="Maintain a Running Commentary">
        Call out every turn, speed change, and cross street. Keep transmissions short and clear. Dispatch will coordinate secondary units and notify supervisors. Do not key your radio unless you have positional information to relay — silence is preferable to broken traffic.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Follow Supervisor Guidance">
        A supervisor or dispatch may authorise a tactical intervention (spike strip, PIT manoeuvre) or order termination. Comply immediately. Do not freelance tactical deployments without authorisation.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Terminate or Contain">
        If authorised to terminate, disengage and broadcast your last known direction of travel so air or secondary ground units can attempt to locate. If the pursuit ends in a stop, secure the scene before approaching — do not rush the vehicle alone.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Warning>
      **Do not pursue when any of the following apply:**
      - Heavy pedestrian or vehicle traffic creates an unreasonable risk of injury to bystanders
      - Weather or road conditions (rain, construction zones, school zones) significantly increase danger
      - The suspect's identity is confirmed and an arrest can be made later without public risk
      - A supervisor has ordered the pursuit terminated
      - Your unit is the only one available and you are handling a higher-priority call

      Terminating a pursuit is not a failure — it is sound judgement. Document your decision in your report.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Scene Management">
    Arriving first on scene means you are responsible for establishing control until a supervisor or the appropriate investigative unit takes over. Follow the sequence below to keep the scene safe and intact.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Announce Your Arrival and Initial Observations">
        Radio dispatch with your arrival, a quick scene description, and any immediate needs — fire, medical, additional units.

        ```text
        "Dispatch, Unit 4-Lincoln-12 on scene at 1200 Baker Avenue — 
        one vehicle vs. structure, unknown injuries, requesting Fire and EMS."
        ```
      </Step>
      <Step title="Establish a Perimeter">
        Use your unit to block approaching traffic if needed. Set out road flares or cones if available. Keep bystanders and non-involved parties well back from the immediate scene. The perimeter should be wide enough to preserve any physical evidence.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Render Aid and Assess Hazards">
        Check for injured parties and provide first aid within your training. Identify any hazards — fuel leaks, downed power lines, armed subjects — and communicate them to incoming units and dispatch before they arrive.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Wait for Backup Before Escalating">
        Do not enter a structure, vehicle, or confined space alone if a threat may be present. Hold your position, maintain radio contact, and wait for a secondary unit unless someone's life is in immediate danger and you have no alternative.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Hand Off to Investigators or Supervisors">
        When a detective, supervisor, or specialist unit (K9, ERT, Traffic Homicide) arrives, provide a verbal briefing: what you observed on arrival, what actions you have taken, and the current status of any subjects. Do not leave the scene until you are formally released by the incoming authority.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Document and Clear">
        Complete your scene notes while details are fresh. File your preliminary report in Imperial CAD before end of shift. Reference [Ten-Codes](/communication/ten-codes) for correct status codes when clearing.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Note>
      If you are the primary unit on a scene that becomes a criminal investigation, avoid moving, touching, or disturbing any physical items beyond what is required for safety or medical aid. Preserve the scene for investigators.
    </Note>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
