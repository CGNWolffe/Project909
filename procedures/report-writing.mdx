Every enforcement action you take — traffic stop, arrest, use of force, or major incident — requires a written record in Imperial CAD. A well-written report protects you, supports any follow-on prosecution, and gives supervisors and investigators the full picture long after you have left the scene. Reports should always be professional, detailed, factual, and written in strict chronological order. This page covers the standard format, the six-section narrative structure, a complete worked example, and a final checklist to review before you submit.

## Standard Report Format

When creating a new report in Imperial CAD, fill out every header field before writing your narrative. Leaving fields blank forces supervisors to return the report for correction.

```text
Date/Time:
Location:
Officer(s) Involved:
Agency:
Incident Type:
Suspect(s):
Victim(s):
Vehicle(s):
Charges:
Evidence:
Narrative:
```

<Tip>
  Vehicle descriptions must include **make, model, colour, and plate number**. Names must include full first and last name as confirmed by identification — never use nicknames or shorthand in a formal report.
</Tip>

## Report Writing Rules

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use Professional Language">
    Write as if a judge, defence attorney, or review board will read every word — because they might. Avoid slang, abbreviations, humour, and personal opinions. Use correct law enforcement terminology throughout.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write in Chronological Order">
    Begin with your first observation and continue forward in time until the scene is cleared. Do not jump ahead to the outcome and then circle back to explain how you got there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Include All Relevant Observations">
    Record what you saw, heard, smelled, or detected. Sensory observations are foundational to establishing probable cause and justifying enforcement actions — spell them out explicitly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document Every Officer Action">
    Traffic stops, arrests, searches, citations, use of force, and evidence collection must all appear in the narrative. If you did it on the scene, it belongs in the report.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Explain the Outcome">
    State clearly how the incident concluded: subject arrested and transported, citation issued and subject released, scene turned over to investigators, etc.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Narrative Structure

A complete narrative is built from six sections, written in order. Each section has a defined purpose — do not skip or merge them.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1. Initial Observation" icon="eye">
    Open by explaining where you were, what you were doing, and what drew your attention to the subject or incident. This establishes your lawful presence and the basis for your initial contact.

    > *"On [date] at approximately [time] hours, I was on patrol in the area of [location] when I observed..."*
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="2. Suspect Actions" icon="person-running">
    Describe what the suspect, vehicle, or involved party was doing that prompted your response. Be specific and factual — record observable behaviour, not inferred intent.

    > *"The vehicle was travelling at a high rate of speed... The driver failed to maintain lane... The suspect fled on foot northbound..."*
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="3. Officer Response" icon="shield">
    Explain the actions you took in direct response to what you observed. Include activation of emergency equipment, commands given, positions taken, and any radio transmissions to dispatch.

    > *"I activated my emergency lights and siren and conducted a traffic stop near the intersection of..."*
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="4. Investigation" icon="magnifying-glass">
    Document everything that occurred during your investigation: contact with subjects, statements made, observations of impairment or evidence, field sobriety tests, consent or warrant-based searches, and evidence collected.

    > *"While speaking with the driver, I observed signs of impairment, including slurred speech, bloodshot eyes, and the odour of alcohol..."*
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="5. Enforcement Action" icon="handcuffs">
    State the enforcement decision and the basis for it. List all charges, citations, or warnings issued. If an arrest was made, include the legal basis and the method of transport.

    > *"Based on my observations and the results of the field sobriety investigation, the driver was placed under arrest for impaired operation of a motor vehicle..."*
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="6. Scene Conclusion" icon="flag-checkered">
    Conclude with the final disposition of all subjects, vehicles, and evidence. Note when the scene was cleared and any handoffs to other units or agencies.

    > *"The suspect was searched incident to arrest and transported to the station for processing. The vehicle was towed from the scene without incident."*
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Complete Example: Traffic Stop Report

The following is a full Imperial CAD report entry for a DUI traffic stop. Use this as a reference for format, tone, and level of detail.

```text
Date/Time:       05/22/2026 — 2135 hours
Location:        Route 68 at Joshua Road, San Bernardino County
Officer(s):      Deputy J. Wolffe, Badge #4112
Agency:          San Bernardino County Sheriff's Department
Incident Type:   Traffic Stop — DUI
Suspect(s):      John Smith, M/W, DOB 01/15/1990
Victim(s):       N/A
Vehicle(s):      2019 Dodge Charger, Black, CA Plate 7AFR904
Charges:         Impaired Operation of a Motor Vehicle (VC 23152)
Evidence:        Field sobriety test results — failed all three standardized tests
Narrative:

On 05/22/2026 at approximately 2135 hours, I was on patrol in the area of Route 68
when I observed a black Dodge Charger travelling at a high rate of speed.

I activated my emergency lights and siren and conducted a traffic stop near the
intersection of Route 68 and Joshua Road. The vehicle came to a stop on the right
shoulder. I advised dispatch of the stop prior to exiting my unit.

I approached the driver side of the vehicle and made contact with the driver, later
identified as John Smith via California Driver Licence. I advised the driver of the
reason for the stop and requested their driver's licence, registration, and proof of
insurance.

While speaking with the driver, I observed signs of impairment, including slurred
speech, bloodshot and watery eyes, and the odour of an alcoholic beverage emanating
from the vehicle interior.

The driver was asked to exit the vehicle and perform standardized field sobriety
tests. The driver was unable to complete the Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus, Walk-and-Turn,
or One-Leg Stand tests without observable impairment indicators.

Based on my observations and the results of the field sobriety investigation, the
driver was placed under arrest for impaired operation of a motor vehicle. The suspect
was searched incident to arrest and transported to the station for processing. The
vehicle was towed from the scene without incident. The scene was cleared at 2218 hours.
```

## Good vs. Bad Reports

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Good Report" icon="badge-check" href="/procedures/report-writing#complete-example-traffic-stop-report">
    ```text
    On 05/22/2026 at approximately 2010 hours, I observed
    a white Ford Explorer fail to stop at a posted stop
    sign near the intersection of Bay Street and Lakeshore
    Road, San Bernardino County.

    I initiated a traffic stop using my emergency lights
    and siren. The vehicle came to a stop without incident
    on the right shoulder of Bay Street.

    I contacted the driver, identified as Maria Torres via
    California Driver Licence. I issued citation #BC-4892
    for failure to stop at a posted stop sign (VC 22450).
    The driver was released at the scene. The stop was
    cleared at 2021 hours.
    ```
  </Card>
  <Card title="Bad Report" icon="triangle-exclamation" href="/procedures/report-writing#report-writing-rules">
    ```text
    Pulled guy over. Ran stop sign. Gave ticket.
    ```

    This report is missing:
    - Date and time
    - Exact location
    - Driver identification
    - Vehicle information
    - Citation number and specific charge
    - Officer actions and emergency equipment use
    - Scene clearance time
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Common Professional Report Phrases

Keep your language consistent across all reports by drawing on established law enforcement phrasing.

```text
"I observed..."
"Based on my observations..."
"The suspect stated..."
"I detected the odour of..."
"I activated my emergency equipment (lights and siren)..."
"The vehicle failed to yield to my emergency lights and siren..."
"The suspect was placed under arrest for..."
"The suspect was searched incident to arrest..."
"No further evidence was located during the search."
"The vehicle was towed from the scene by [tow company]."
"The scene was cleared at [time] hours without further incident."
"The investigation was turned over to [unit/agency] upon their arrival."
"The suspect was transported to [facility] for processing."
```

## Final Report Checklist

Review every point below before submitting your report in Imperial CAD.

<Note>
  **Before you submit, confirm your report includes:**

  - Date and exact time of the incident
  - Precise location (road name, intersection, address, or landmark)
  - Your name, badge number, and agency
  - Full name and identifying information for all suspects and victims
  - Complete vehicle description (make, model, colour, plate)
  - Clear reason for initial contact or response
  - All officer actions documented in chronological order
  - Sensory observations used to establish probable cause
  - All charges, citations, or warnings issued with applicable codes
  - Evidence collected and how it was secured
  - Final disposition of all subjects and vehicles
  - Scene clearance time and any handoff to another unit or agency
</Note>
